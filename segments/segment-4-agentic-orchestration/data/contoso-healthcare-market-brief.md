# Healthcare Logistics Robotics -- Market Brief

**Prepared for:** Contoso Robotics Executive Team
**Date:** April 2026

## Market Size and Growth

The global healthcare logistics robotics market was valued at approximately $1.8 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach $3.4 billion by 2029, representing a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 17.2%. North America accounts for roughly 42% of the market, driven by hospital labor shortages, rising operational costs, and pressure to reduce medication delivery errors.

The primary application is autonomous material transport within hospitals: delivering medications from pharmacy to nursing stations, moving lab specimens, transporting sterile supplies, and returning soiled linens. Secondary applications include inter-building transport on large medical campuses and last-mile delivery within outpatient clinic networks.

## Key Competitors

**Aethon (ST Engineering):** Market leader with the TUG autonomous mobile robot, deployed in over 500 hospitals globally. Strong relationships with hospital group purchasing organizations (GPOs). Acquired by ST Engineering in 2020, giving them deep pockets and international distribution.

**Fetch Robotics (Zebra Technologies):** Originally focused on warehouse automation, Fetch expanded into healthcare after its 2021 acquisition by Zebra. Leverages Zebra's existing hospital infrastructure relationships (badge printers, asset tracking). Competitive on price but less specialized for clinical environments.

**Locus Robotics:** Primarily a warehouse automation company with emerging interest in healthcare. Strong collaborative robot (cobot) capabilities. Has not yet made significant hospital deployments but holds relevant patents in autonomous navigation in crowded environments.

## Regulatory Considerations

Hospital robotics must comply with multiple overlapping frameworks:

- **FDA:** Autonomous transport robots used in clinical settings may require 510(k) clearance depending on whether they transport regulated medical devices or pharmaceuticals.
- **HIPAA:** Robots with cameras or sensors that could capture patient information must have data handling controls and encryption.
- **Joint Commission / CMS:** Hospitals must demonstrate that robotic systems do not create safety hazards in corridors, patient rooms, or elevator systems.
- **UL 3100 / ISO 3691-4:** Safety standards for autonomous industrial vehicles, increasingly referenced in hospital procurement requirements.

Regulatory timelines for 510(k) clearance average 6-12 months. Hospital procurement cycles add another 6-18 months from initial evaluation to purchase order.
